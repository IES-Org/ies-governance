# Proposal Lifecycle

This document defines the lifecycle for proposals within IES, the Information Exchange Standard.

The lifecycle provides a consistent route for moving proposals from initial idea through to review, decision, implementation, and closure.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of the proposal lifecycle is to ensure that proposals are handled transparently, proportionately, and through the appropriate governance route.

The lifecycle supports:

* clear proposal status
* consistent routing
* proportionate review
* traceable decision-making
* clear implementation responsibilities
* appropriate closure of proposal records

---

## Lifecycle Overview

A proposal may move through the following lifecycle stages:

* Draft
* Submitted
* Triage
* Under Review
* Returned for Further Information
* Accepted
* Accepted with Conditions
* Deferred
* Rejected
* Implemented
* Superseded
* Closed

Not every proposal will pass through every stage. The route depends on the nature, scope, and impact of the proposal.

---

## Draft

A proposal is in Draft while it is being prepared.

At this stage, the proposer should use the [Proposal Template](./proposal-template.md) to describe the proposed change, rationale, expected impact, affected repositories or groups, and decision required.

A Draft proposal may be discussed informally with relevant Domain Working Groups, L0 or L1 Ontology Maintainers, Platform and Communications Management, Repository Maintainers, or Steering Group members before submission.

---

## Submitted

A proposal becomes Submitted when it is sent for consideration.

Completed proposals should be submitted to:

```text
administrator@informationexchangestandard.org
```

Where appropriate, the proposal may also be linked to relevant GitHub issues, pull requests, repository discussions, Domain Working Group records, or Steering Group agenda items.

Submission does not mean that the proposal has been accepted. It means the proposal is ready for routing and consideration.

---

## Triage

Triage determines the appropriate route for the proposal.

During triage, the proposal should be assessed to identify:

* whether the proposal is complete enough to review
* what type of proposal it is
* which repositories, groups, roles, or processes are affected
* whether the proposal is routine, material, urgent, disputed, or exceptional
* which governance group or role-holders should review it
* whether Steering Group consideration is required
* whether the proposal should be linked to a GitHub issue, pull request, or other record

Triage may be carried out by the administrator, the relevant governance group, or the Steering Group, depending on the nature of the proposal.

---

## Under Review

A proposal is Under Review when it is being considered by the relevant people or governance group.

Review may involve:

* the relevant Domain Working Group
* L0 or L1 Ontology Maintainers
* Repository Maintainers
* Platform and Communications Management
* the Steering Group
* other relevant subject matter experts or affected parties

Review should be proportionate to the significance of the proposal.

A proposal affecting IES Top or IES Core should involve the L0 or L1 Ontology Maintainers. A proposal with strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, disputed, or exceptional implications must be escalated to the Steering Group.

---

## Returned for Further Information

A proposal may be Returned for Further Information where it cannot be assessed properly in its current form.

This may happen where:

* the proposal is unclear
* the rationale is incomplete
* the expected impact is not described
* affected repositories, groups, or processes are not identified
* technical implications are not sufficiently understood
* implementation requirements are missing
* risks, dependencies, or security implications need further explanation
* the decision required is unclear

A returned proposal may be revised and resubmitted.

---

## Accepted

A proposal is Accepted where the relevant decision-making route agrees that the proposal should proceed.

Acceptance should identify:

* what has been accepted
* who approved the proposal
* any implementation route
* any affected repositories, documents, roles, or processes
* any required records or updates
* any public release implications

Acceptance of a proposal does not itself implement the change.

Implementation must take place through the appropriate repository, process, role, or governance mechanism.

---

## Accepted with Conditions

A proposal may be Accepted with Conditions where the proposal is approved subject to specific requirements.

Conditions may relate to:

* technical changes
* review requirements
* wording changes
* repository documentation
* security checks
* public release checks
* implementation sequencing
* Steering Group approval
* Domain Working Group review
* L0 or L1 Ontology Maintainer review
* Platform and Communications action

Conditions should be documented clearly so that implementation can be checked against them.

---

## Deferred

A proposal may be Deferred where a decision should not yet be made.

This may happen where:

* further evidence is needed
* dependencies need to be resolved
* the timing is not appropriate
* a related proposal should be considered first
* the relevant Domain Working Group has not yet been formed
* the proposal is better considered as part of a wider review
* resourcing, funding, or operational capacity is not yet available

A deferred proposal should record why it has been deferred and, where possible, what would allow it to be reconsidered.

---

## Rejected

A proposal is Rejected where the relevant decision-making route decides that it should not proceed.

A rejection should record the reason for the decision.

Reasons may include:

* the proposal is inconsistent with the IES Charter
* the proposal creates unacceptable technical, governance, security, or operational risk
* the proposal lacks sufficient rationale or evidence
* the proposal is outside the scope of IES
* the proposal duplicates existing work
* the proposal would create misleading domain authority or repository naming
* the proposal lacks the required domain, technical, or organisational support
* the proposal is not proportionate to the expected benefit

A rejected proposal may be superseded by a new proposal if circumstances change or if a materially different proposal is submitted.

---

## Implemented

A proposal is Implemented when the accepted change has been carried out through the appropriate route.

Implementation may include:

* merging a pull request
* updating governance documentation
* updating repository-level documentation
* updating the Ontology Index
* updating membership records
* creating, renaming, archiving, or publishing a repository
* changing access or platform arrangements
* recording a Steering Group decision
* preparing or completing a public release

Implementation should remain traceable to the accepted proposal.

---

## Superseded

A proposal is Superseded where it has been replaced by another proposal, decision, or governance record.

The superseded proposal should identify the proposal, decision, or record that replaces it where possible.

A superseded proposal should not normally be deleted, because it may still provide useful context and traceability.

---

## Closed

A proposal is Closed when no further action is required.

Closure may occur because the proposal has been:

* implemented
* rejected
* withdrawn
* superseded
* deferred indefinitely
* resolved through another route

The closure record should make clear why the proposal was closed and where any relevant follow-up information can be found.

---

## Decision Records

Proposal decisions should be recorded in an appropriate place.

Depending on the proposal, this may include:

* Steering Group minutes
* Domain Working Group records
* GitHub issues
* pull requests
* governance decision records
* release notes
* changelog entries
* repository-level documentation

Material decisions should record:

* the proposal considered
* the decision made
* who made the decision
* any conditions
* any implementation route
* any follow-up actions

---

## Implementation Records

Implementation records should show how an accepted proposal was carried out.

Depending on the change, implementation records may include:

* linked issues
* linked pull requests
* merged commits
* updated Markdown files
* updated repository settings
* updated maintainer files
* updated release records
* updated public documentation

Where possible, implementation records should link back to the accepted proposal or decision record.

---

## Escalation

A proposal must be escalated where the relevant working level cannot decide it or where the proposal has wider implications.

Escalation may be required where the proposal:

* affects IES Top or IES Core
* has cross-domain implications
* affects governance arrangements
* affects public release
* affects access, security, legal, or operational risk
* creates a dispute that cannot be resolved at the relevant working level
* requires an exception or waiver
* requires Steering Group decision

Where escalation is required, the proposal should be placed on the appropriate Steering Group agenda or routed through the agreed urgent route.

---

## Urgent Proposals

Some proposals may require urgent consideration.

Urgency may arise where there is:

* a security concern
* a risk to access, availability, or operational continuity
* a release issue
* a governance issue that blocks necessary work
* a funding or platform risk
* a significant error in public-facing material

Urgent proposals should still be recorded and traceable, even where they are handled outside the normal meeting cycle.

---

## Status Labels

Where GitHub issues or project boards are used to track proposals, the following status labels may be used:

* `proposal: draft`
* `proposal: submitted`
* `proposal: triage`
* `proposal: under-review`
* `proposal: further-information`
* `proposal: accepted`
* `proposal: accepted-with-conditions`
* `proposal: deferred`
* `proposal: rejected`
* `proposal: implemented`
* `proposal: superseded`
* `proposal: closed`

Repositories may use additional labels where helpful, provided the proposal status remains clear.

---

## Relationship with Proposal Template

The proposal lifecycle should be used alongside the [Proposal Template](./proposal-template.md).

The template helps ensure that proposals contain the information needed for triage, review, decision-making, implementation, and closure.

# Steering Group Membership

This document defines the membership model for the IES Steering Group.

It describes how the Steering Group is formed, the distinction between Voting Members and Advisory Members, how the Steering Group Chair is appointed, and the expectations that apply to Steering Group members.

---

## Membership Model

The Steering Group is composed of:

* Voting Members
* Advisory Members

Voting Members hold formal decision-making rights within the Steering Group. Advisory Members contribute specialist technical expertise and advice but do not hold voting rights.

Voting Members join the Steering Group automatically because they are either Domain Working Group Chairs or Domain Working Group Representatives.

Advisory Members are appointed by the Steering Group.

---

## Voting Members

Voting Members are responsible for formal decision-making within the Steering Group.

Each Domain Working Group has two Voting Member positions on the Steering Group:

* the Domain Working Group Chair
* one Domain Working Group Representative

Each Voting Member has one vote.

Voting Members are expected to represent the position and interests of their Domain Working Group while also acting in the interests of IES as a whole.

---

## Domain Working Group Chairs

The appointment, replacement, or removal of a Domain Working Group Chair is managed by the relevant Domain Working Group through its own agreed arrangements.

The Steering Group does not appoint Domain Working Group Chairs.

---

## Domain Working Group Representatives

Each Domain Working Group Chair may appoint one Domain Working Group Representative.

The Representative role provides resilience and continuity for the Domain Working Group’s participation in Steering Group activity. It also allows the Chair to appoint someone with complementary expertise or availability.

A Domain Working Group may choose for both the Chair and Representative to attend Steering Group meetings, but it is not necessary for both to attend every meeting.

A Domain Working Group Chair may replace their Representative at any point.

---

## Advisory Members

Advisory Members participate in Steering Group discussions but do not hold voting rights.

The Steering Group may appoint up to two Advisory Members.

Advisory Members are appointed for their technical expertise in ontology development and their ability to advise on the technical integrity of IES. They must be members of an active Domain Working Group.

Advisory Members should have a strong understanding of:

* ontology engineering
* 4D modelling principles
* IES Top
* IES Core
* IES Common
* domain-driven extensions
* the impact of proposed changes on technical coherence and interoperability

Advisory Members are not appointed to represent their Domain Working Group. They provide technical advice, identify implications for IES Common and domain-driven development, and support informed decision-making by the Steering Group.

Both Advisory Member positions cannot be filled by people from the same Domain Working Group.

---

## Steering Group Chair

The Steering Group is led by a Chair.

The Steering Group Chair must be:

* an existing Domain Working Group Chair
* a public sector representative

The Steering Group Chair is appointed by majority vote of the Voting Members.

The Chair continues to hold the rights and responsibilities of a Domain Working Group Chair while performing the additional role of Steering Group Chair.

The Chair is responsible for supporting the effective and impartial operation of the Steering Group. This includes organising meetings, managing agendas, facilitating discussion, supporting consensus, and ensuring that decisions and relevant actions are documented.

---

## Appointment

### Voting Members

Voting Members join the Steering Group through their Domain Working Group role.

### Advisory Members

Advisory Members are appointed by majority vote of the Voting Members.

### Steering Group Chair

The Steering Group Chair is appointed by majority vote of the Voting Members.

Candidates must be existing Domain Working Group Chairs and public sector representatives.

The term of the Chair is agreed at the time of appointment.

---

## Participation Expectations

Steering Group members are expected to participate actively and constructively.

Members should:

* attend Steering Group meetings where reasonably possible
* review materials in advance of decision-making
* contribute expertise and judgement
* act in accordance with the IES Charter
* consider both their represented area and the interests of IES as a whole
* declare relevant conflicts of interest
* support transparent and traceable decision-making

Voting Members should be able to represent the position and interests of their Domain Working Group. Advisory Members should provide technically grounded advice in the interests of the integrity of IES.

---

## Term and Review

A Voting Member’s term is linked to their Domain Working Group role.

A Domain Working Group Chair remains a Voting Member while they remain Chair of an active Domain Working Group. A Domain Working Group Representative remains a Voting Member while they remain appointed by the relevant Domain Working Group Chair.

Advisory Member appointments should be reviewed periodically to ensure that the Steering Group continues to have access to appropriate technical expertise.

The Steering Group Chair’s term is agreed at the time of appointment by majority vote of the Voting Members. The term is typically two years, but a different period may be agreed as part of the appointment decision.

The agreed term should be documented in the Steering Group minutes.

A review and appointment vote should be prepared for the final Steering Group meeting before the end of the Chair’s term.

The Steering Group Chair’s term should end if the Chair is no longer a Domain Working Group Chair, is no longer a public sector representative, reaches the end of their agreed term, or is replaced through a majority vote of the Voting Members.

---

## Changes in Membership

Changes in Steering Group membership should be documented in the relevant membership records.

Current members are recorded in:

* [Current Voting Members](./voting-members.md)
* [Current Advisory Members](./advisory-members.md)

Historic membership is recorded in:

* [Past Steering Group Members](./past-members.md)

Where a person becomes or ceases to be a Domain Working Group Chair, the Steering Group membership records should be updated.

Where a Domain Working Group Representative is appointed or replaced by the relevant Domain Working Group Chair, the Steering Group membership records should be updated.

Where an Advisory Member is appointed, replaced, or ceases to serve, the Advisory Member records should be updated.

---

## Conflicts of Interest

Steering Group members should declare any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest in relation to matters under consideration.

A conflict of interest does not necessarily prevent participation, but it should be made transparent and managed appropriately. Where necessary, the Steering Group may decide that a member should not participate in a particular decision.

---

## Removal or Replacement

A Domain Working Group Chair ceases to be a Voting Member when they cease to be Chair of an active Domain Working Group.

A Domain Working Group Representative ceases to be a Voting Member when they are replaced by the relevant Domain Working Group Chair or when the relevant Domain Working Group is no longer active.

An Advisory Member may be removed or replaced by majority vote of the Voting Members where they:

* are no longer a member of an active Domain Working Group
* are no longer able to provide the required technical advice
* are unable to participate for a sustained period
* have a conflict of interest that cannot be managed appropriately
* materially fail to act in accordance with the IES Charter or governance processes

Removal or replacement should be documented in the relevant membership records.

---

## Public Record

The Steering Group maintains a public record of current and past membership.

The public record identifies current Voting Members, current Advisory Members, and past Steering Group members. This supports transparency, accountability, and continuity of governance.

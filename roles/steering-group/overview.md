# Steering Group Overview

The Steering Group provides strategic oversight and governance direction for IES, the Information Exchange Standard.

It is responsible for ensuring that IES remains aligned with its purpose, principles, public interest obligations, and long-term direction. The Steering Group acts as the principal governance body for matters that have strategic, cross-domain, or significant technical impact.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of the Steering Group is to provide clear and accountable governance for IES.

The Steering Group supports the development and maintenance of IES by:

* setting strategic direction
* overseeing governance arrangements
* supporting cross-domain coherence
* approving major changes to IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1)
* resolving escalated or disputed matters
* ensuring that decisions remain aligned with the IES Charter

The Steering Group is not responsible for undertaking day-to-day domain development or routine repository maintenance. Those responsibilities sit with Domain Working Groups, Repository Maintainers, IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainerss, and other relevant technical support and maintenance functions.

---

## Scope of Responsibility

The Steering Group is responsible for decisions and oversight relating to the overall direction and governance of IES.

This includes:

* strategic direction and prioritisation
* interpretation and application of the IES Charter
* approval of major changes to IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1)
* cross-domain alignment and harmonisation
* escalation of unresolved disputes
* oversight of governance roles and governance processes
* approval of material changes to governance arrangements
* oversight of public release arrangements where strategic approval is required

The Steering Group should normally consider issues at the level of principle, governance, strategic impact, or cross-domain consequence. It should not routinely act as a substitute for domain-level decision-making or repository-level maintenance.

---

## Composition

The Steering Group consists of voting members and advisory members, details of which are set out in [Steering Group Membership](./membership.md).

---

## Decision-Making

Decision-making within the Steering Group should be transparent, proportionate, and aligned with the IES Charter.

The Steering Group should seek consensus where possible. Where consensus cannot be reached, decisions may be determined by a majority vote of voting members.

Voting is undertaken by voting members only. Advisory members may contribute to discussion and provide recommendations, but they do not participate in formal voting.

Where required, the Steering Group Chair may cast a tie-breaker vote.

---

## Matters Requiring Steering Group Decision

The following matters should normally be considered by the Steering Group:

* major changes to IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1)
* changes with significant cross-domain impact
* changes affecting the governance model or governance roles
* public release decisions requiring strategic approval
* unresolved disputes escalated from Domain Working Groups or technical support and maintenance functions
* questions concerning interpretation of the IES Charter
* appointment or confirmation of certain governance role-holders where required by the governance documentation

Not every change to IES requires Steering Group decision. Routine repository-level activity, domain-specific development, and minor technical maintenance should normally be handled at the lowest appropriate governance level.

---

## Relationship with Domain Working Groups

Domain Working Groups lead domain-driven development and provide subject matter expertise for their respective domains.

The Steering Group provides strategic oversight and a route for escalation. It does not normally direct the day-to-day work of Domain Working Groups, but it may consider matters raised by them where there is strategic significance, cross-domain impact, or a need for formal governance decision.

Each Domain Working Group is represented on the Steering Group by its Chair and Representative.

---

## Relationship with IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainerss

IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainerss are responsible for maintaining IES Top and IES Core, which together form IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1).

The Steering Group relies on the IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainerss for technical advice on changes affecting IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1). IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainerss may identify technical implications, recommend courses of action, and enact approved changes through the appropriate repository processes.

Major changes to IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1) should be reviewed and approved by the Steering Group before implementation.

---

## Relationship with Repository Maintainers

Repository Maintainers are responsible for maintaining specific IES repositories.

Repository Maintainers are not centrally listed in this governance repository. Each repository is expected to maintain its own maintainer file identifying the people responsible for that repository.

Where repository-level issues have strategic, cross-domain, governance, or IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1) implications, they may be escalated to the Steering Group through the appropriate governance route.

---

## Escalation

Issues should be resolved at the lowest appropriate governance level.

Escalation to the Steering Group may occur where:

* a matter has strategic or cross-domain implications
* a proposed change affects IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1)
* a dispute cannot be resolved within a Domain Working Group
* a technical matter cannot be resolved through the relevant maintenance route
* a governance decision or interpretation is required

The Steering Group acts as the final escalation point within the IES governance structure.

---

## Records and Transparency

Steering Group decisions should be documented through the appropriate governance records.

Records may include meeting notes, decision logs, issues, pull requests, or other governance artefacts maintained within the IES GitHub organisation.

The level of documentation should be proportionate to the significance of the decision. Material decisions should be traceable from discussion and approval through to implementation where relevant.

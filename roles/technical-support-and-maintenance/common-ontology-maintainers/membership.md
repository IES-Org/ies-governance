# Common Ontology Maintainer Membership

This document defines the membership model for Common Ontology Maintainers.

It describes who may become a Common Ontology Maintainer, how Common Ontology Maintainers are appointed, the expectations that apply to the role, and how current and past Common Ontology Maintainers are recorded.

---

## Membership Model

Common Ontology Maintainers are responsible for maintaining IES Top and IES Core.

Because changes to IES Top and IES Core may affect all domain-driven repositories, the Common Ontology Maintainer role is limited to a small group of suitably qualified individuals.

Common Ontology Maintainers are appointed to provide technical stewardship of IES Top and IES Core. They are expected to act in the interests of IES as a whole, not in the interests of any single domain, organisation, or repository.

Common Ontology Maintainers share collective responsibility for IES Top and IES Core. Individual maintainers may take lead responsibility for particular areas of expertise, but lead responsibilities do not create exclusive ownership of any part of IES Top or IES Core.

---

## Eligibility

To become a Common Ontology Maintainer, an individual must have the technical expertise, judgement, and practical capability required to maintain IES Top and IES Core within the IES GitHub organisation.

A Common Ontology Maintainer should have strong understanding of:

* ontology engineering
* foundational ontology
* 4D modelling principles
* extensional ontology principles
* IES Top
* IES Core
* RDF ontology implementation
* domain-driven extensions
* GitHub-based version control, issue management, pull requests, branching, review workflows, and repository-based development
* repository access controls and the responsible use of elevated permissions
* the impact of ontology changes on interoperability and maintainability
* the relationship between conceptual models and implementation artefacts

The Common Ontology Maintainer group should collectively have sufficient expertise in:

* public sector and cross-government requirements
* foundational ontology
* RDF ontology implementation
* GitHub-based development and review
* repository access controls, branching strategies, pull request review, and release preparation
* licensing, security, and release considerations relevant to IES Top and IES Core
* the relationship between IES Top, IES Core, and domain-driven extensions

A Common Ontology Maintainer must be able to assess proposed changes in the context of IES as a whole and must be able to work effectively within the GitHub-based development model used by IES.

---

## Appointment

Common Ontology Maintainers are appointed by majority vote of the Steering Group.

A candidate may be proposed where there is a clear need for additional Common Ontology Maintainer capacity and where the candidate has the required technical expertise and practical GitHub capability.

Appointments should consider both the suitability of the individual candidate and the collective capability of the Common Ontology Maintainer group.

The appointment should be documented in the relevant Steering Group records and reflected in [Current Common Ontology Maintainers](./current-common-ontology-maintainers.md).

---

## Number of Common Ontology Maintainers

The number of Common Ontology Maintainers should remain proportionate to the needs of IES.

The role is intentionally restricted because it carries responsibility for IES Top and IES Core. The number of Common Ontology Maintainers should be sufficient to provide resilience, continuity, effective review, and practical repository maintenance, but not so large that accountability or technical coherence is weakened.

---

## Responsibilities of Members

Common Ontology Maintainers are expected to:

* maintain the structure and integrity of IES Top and IES Core
* review proposed changes affecting IES Top and IES Core
* identify technical implications of proposed changes
* ensure that changes remain consistent with ontology engineering principles and 4D modelling
* support compatibility between IES Top, IES Core, and domain-driven extensions
* support traceability between conceptual models, implementation artefacts, and approved repository content
* use GitHub effectively to review issues, assess pull requests, manage branches, support review workflows, and maintain traceable development activity
* implement approved changes through the appropriate repository processes
* document material changes where required
* support public release activity relating to IES Top and IES Core
* escalate strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, or disputed matters to the Steering Group

Common Ontology Maintainers must act in accordance with the IES Charter and relevant governance processes.

---

## Authority and Limits

Common Ontology Maintainers have authority to maintain IES Top and IES Core within the limits of the IES governance framework.

They may undertake routine maintenance activity where this does not materially alter the structure, meaning, or cross-domain behaviour of IES Top or IES Core.

Material changes must follow the relevant proposal, review, and approval process.

Changes with strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, or disputed implications must be escalated to the Steering Group.

The Common Ontology Maintainer role does not automatically grant authority over domain-driven repositories. Domain-driven repositories are governed by the relevant Domain Working Group and maintained through their own repository-level arrangements.

The role requires sufficient GitHub capability to exercise repository permissions responsibly. Repository permissions must not be treated as a substitute for governance approval.

---

## Access and Permissions

Common Ontology Maintainers should have access and permissions appropriate to maintaining IES Top and IES Core.

Access must be proportionate to the role and must be reviewed when a Common Ontology Maintainer leaves the role, no longer requires access, or where there is a relevant governance, security, or operational reason to review permissions.

Access and permissions must be consistent with repository-level security and governance requirements.

Common Ontology Maintainers must understand the implications of the repository permissions they hold, including the effect of branch protection, review requirements, merge permissions, release permissions, and any restrictions applied to protected branches.

---

## Participation Expectations

Common Ontology Maintainers are expected to participate actively and constructively.

They should:

* review relevant proposals and pull requests in a timely manner
* provide technically grounded advice
* consider the impact of changes across IES
* support domain-driven development where changes interact with IES Top or IES Core
* use GitHub effectively to support issue management, branching, pull request review, and traceable implementation
* follow the agreed branching, review, approval, and release practices for the relevant repository
* declare relevant conflicts of interest
* support transparent and traceable decision-making
* maintain appropriate records of material changes

---

## Conflicts of Interest

Common Ontology Maintainers should declare any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest in relation to matters under consideration.

A conflict of interest does not necessarily prevent participation, but it must be made transparent and managed appropriately. Where necessary, the relevant governance route may determine that a Common Ontology Maintainer should not participate in a particular decision or implementation activity.

---

## Review of Membership

Common Ontology Maintainer membership should be reviewed periodically to ensure that the role remains accurate, proportionate, and fit for purpose.

A review may consider:

* whether there is sufficient maintainer capacity
* whether the current maintainers continue to meet the technical expectations of the role
* whether the current maintainers continue to meet the GitHub capability expectations of the role
* whether the group collectively has the right mix of expertise
* whether access and permissions remain appropriate
* whether additional appointments are required
* whether any maintainer should step down or be replaced

Any material change to Common Ontology Maintainer membership must be recorded in the relevant public records.

---

## Removal or Replacement

A Common Ontology Maintainer may step down from the role at any point.

A Common Ontology Maintainer may be removed or replaced by majority vote of the Steering Group where they:

* are no longer able to perform the role
* no longer require the role
* no longer meet the technical expectations of the role
* no longer meet the GitHub capability expectations of the role
* are unable to participate for a sustained period
* have a conflict of interest that cannot be managed appropriately
* materially fail to act in accordance with the IES Charter or governance processes

Access and permissions must be reviewed when a Common Ontology Maintainer leaves the role.

---

Current Common Ontology Maintainers are recorded in:

* [Current Common Ontology Maintainers](./current-common-ontology-maintainers.md)

Past Common Ontology Maintainers are recorded in:

# Common Ontology Maintainers Overview

Common Ontology Maintainers are responsible for maintaining IES Top and IES Core.

IES Top and IES Core together form IES Common, the shared foundation used across domain-driven development in IES. Because changes to IES Common may affect multiple domains, the Common Ontology Maintainer role is tightly governed and limited to a small group of suitably qualified individuals.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of the Common Ontology Maintainer role is to protect the technical integrity, coherence, and maintainability of IES Common.

Common Ontology Maintainers support IES by:

* maintaining IES Top and IES Core
* reviewing proposed changes to IES Common
* assessing the impact of changes on domain-driven repositories
* supporting alignment between IES Common and domain-driven development
* implementing approved changes to IES Top and IES Core
* advising on ontology modelling, 4D principles, and technical consistency

---

## Scope of Responsibility

Common Ontology Maintainers are responsible for the maintenance of:

* IES Top
* IES Core

Together, these repositories form IES Common.

The role does not automatically grant maintenance responsibility for domain-driven repositories. Domain-driven repositories are normally maintained through their own repository-level arrangements and governed by the relevant Domain Working Group.

---

## Responsibilities

Common Ontology Maintainers are responsible for:

* maintaining the structure and integrity of IES Top and IES Core
* reviewing proposed changes affecting IES Common
* identifying technical implications of proposed changes
* ensuring that changes remain consistent with ontology engineering principles and 4D modelling
* supporting compatibility between IES Common and domain-driven extensions
* implementing approved changes through the appropriate repository processes
* documenting material changes where required
* supporting public release activity relating to IES Common

Common Ontology Maintainers should act in the interests of IES as a whole, not in the interests of any single domain, organisation, or repository.

---

## Authority

Common Ontology Maintainers have authority to maintain IES Top and IES Core within the limits of the IES governance framework.

They may undertake routine maintenance activity where this does not materially alter the structure, meaning, or cross-domain behaviour of IES Common.

Material changes to IES Common should follow the relevant proposal, review, and approval process. Changes with strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, or disputed implications must be escalated to the Steering Group.

---

## Relationship with the Steering Group

The Steering Group provides strategic oversight and governance direction for IES.

Common Ontology Maintainers advise the Steering Group on the technical implications of proposed changes to IES Common. They may identify risks, recommend technical approaches, and implement changes approved through the appropriate governance route.

The Steering Group must be involved where a proposed change to IES Common has strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, or disputed implications.

---

## Relationship with Domain Working Groups

Domain Working Groups lead domain-driven development within IES.

Common Ontology Maintainers support Domain Working Groups by helping ensure that domain-driven extensions remain aligned with IES Common. Where a Domain Working Group identifies a required change to IES Common, Common Ontology Maintainers should support technical review and advise on the implications of the proposed change.

Domain Working Groups do not directly govern changes to IES Common.

---

## Relationship with Repository Maintainers

Repository Maintainers are responsible for maintaining specific IES repositories.

The Common Ontology Maintainer role is distinct from ordinary repository maintenance. A Repository Maintainer may have permissions for a specific repository, while a Common Ontology Maintainer has responsibility for IES Top and IES Core.

Repository Maintainer information is maintained in the relevant repository’s maintainer file. Common Ontology Maintainer information is maintained centrally in this governance repository.

---

## Technical Expectations

Common Ontology Maintainers should have strong technical expertise in:

* ontology engineering
* 4D modelling principles
* IES Top
* IES Core
* domain-driven extensions
* version control and repository-based development
* the impact of ontology changes on interoperability and maintainability

The role requires the ability to assess technical changes in the context of IES as a whole.

---

## Access and Permissions

Common Ontology Maintainers should have access and permissions appropriate to maintaining IES Top and IES Core.

Access should be proportionate to the role and should be reviewed when a Common Ontology Maintainer leaves the role, no longer requires access, or where there is a relevant governance, security, or operational reason to review permissions.

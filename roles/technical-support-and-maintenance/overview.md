# Technical Support and Maintenance Overview

Technical Support and Maintenance functions support the technical integrity, continuity, accessibility, and management of IES, the Information Exchange Standard.

This area covers the technical roles and responsibilities needed to maintain IES Common, support repository-level development, manage the IES GitHub organisation, and enable effective communication around governance and release activity.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of Technical Support and Maintenance is to ensure that IES remains technically coherent, maintainable, accessible, and appropriately managed.

These functions support the wider governance model by:

* maintaining shared ontology foundations
* supporting repository-level maintenance
* protecting technical consistency across IES
* supporting the operation of the IES GitHub organisation
* enabling clear communication about governance, development, and release activity

---

## Scope of Responsibility

Technical Support and Maintenance includes several distinct types of responsibility.

At a high level, this includes:

* maintaining IES Top and IES Core
* supporting alignment between IES Common and domain-driven repositories
* supporting repository-level development and maintenance
* managing the IES GitHub organisation
* maintaining appropriate access and permissions
* supporting publication, release, and communications activity
* advising on the technical implications of proposed changes

These responsibilities are distributed across specific roles and functions.

---

## Technical Support and Maintenance Functions

Technical Support and Maintenance includes:

* **Common Ontology Maintainers**
* **Repository Maintainers**
* **Platform and Communications Management**

These functions are related, but they are not interchangeable.

---

## Common Ontology Maintainers

Common Ontology Maintainers are responsible for maintaining IES Top and IES Core.

IES Top and IES Core together form IES Common, the shared foundation used across domain-driven development. Changes to IES Common may have consequences across multiple domains and are therefore subject to stricter governance.

Common Ontology Maintainers are centrally documented because they hold a specific and restricted role within IES governance.

## Repository Maintainers

Repository Maintainers are responsible for maintaining specific IES repositories.

Most Repository Maintainers are expected to work within domain-driven repositories. Their permissions and responsibilities are normally limited to the repositories they support.

Repository Maintainers are not centrally listed in this governance repository. Each repository within the IES GitHub organisation is expected to maintain its own repository-level maintainer file, identifying the people responsible for that repository and the relevant maintenance arrangements.

The Ontology Index identifies repositories and provides routes to repository-level information.

## Platform and Communications Management

Platform and Communications Management supports the operation of the IES GitHub organisation and communication around IES governance, development, and release activity.

This includes the GitHub Organisation Custodian role, which supports the management of the IES GitHub organisation, repository access, permissions, and organisational settings.

## Relationship with the Steering Group

The Steering Group provides strategic oversight and governance direction for IES.

Technical Support and Maintenance functions may advise the Steering Group on technical implications, repository management, release readiness, and risks to the technical integrity of IES.

Major changes to IES Common should involve the Common Ontology Maintainers and, where necessary, the Steering Group.

---

## Relationship with Domain Working Groups

Domain Working Groups lead domain-driven development within IES.

Technical Support and Maintenance functions support Domain Working Groups by helping ensure that domain-driven repositories remain aligned with IES Common, relevant development processes, and repository-level expectations.

Repository-level maintenance should normally be handled by the maintainers of the relevant repository.

---

## Relationship with IES Common

IES Common consists of IES Top and IES Core.

Because IES Common provides the shared foundation for domain-driven development, its maintenance is treated as a distinct and tightly governed responsibility.

Changes to IES Common should be considered carefully for their impact on domain-driven repositories, interoperability, technical coherence, and long-term maintainability.

---

## Access and Permissions

Access and permissions should be proportionate to the role being performed.

Common Ontology Maintainers require access appropriate to maintaining IES Top and IES Core. Repository Maintainers require access appropriate to the repositories they maintain. Platform and Communications Management requires access appropriate to operating the IES GitHub organisation and supporting communication activity.

Access should be reviewed when people change roles, leave a role, or no longer require a particular permission.

---

## Escalation

Technical issues should be resolved at the lowest appropriate level.

Escalation may be required where:

* a proposed change affects IES Common
* a technical issue has cross-domain implications
* repository-level maintainers cannot resolve an issue
* access or permissions create governance or security concerns
* a matter requires Steering Group decision
* a release issue affects the integrity or availability of IES

Escalated matters should be taken to the relevant governance group through the appropriate route.

# Charter

This Charter defines the purpose, scope, principles, and governance model for IES, the Information Exchange Standard.

It explains why governance exists, what it covers, and the principles that guide how IES is developed, maintained, and released.

---

## Purpose

IES exists to support consistent information exchange across organisations, systems, assets, sectors, and domains.

The purpose of IES governance is to ensure that IES is developed and maintained in a way that is:

* aligned with public interest
* technically coherent
* transparent and accountable
* usable across domains
* capable of evolving over time

Governance provides the structures, roles, and processes through which changes to IES are proposed, assessed, approved, implemented, and released.

---

## Scope

This Charter applies to the governance arrangements for IES, the Information Exchange Standard.

It covers the governance groups, individual roles, responsibilities, contribution routes, decision-making arrangements, and processes that support the IES initiative.

IES governance includes, for example, the governance of:

* IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1)
* domain-driven repositories
* administrative repositories
* governance groups and individual roles
* contribution, proposal, development, maintenance, and release processes

Repositories within the IES GitHub organisation operate within this governance framework and are expected to maintain repository-level documentation that is consistent with it. Repository-level documentation identifies the purpose of the repository, the responsible parties, the relevant maintenance arrangements, and any repository-specific requirements.

This Charter does not override repository-specific security, access control, assurance, legal, operational, or technical requirements. Contributing organisations remain responsible for their own internal assurance, legal obligations, security requirements, and organisational decision-making.

## Principles

The following principles guide governance activity across IES.

### Public Interest

Governance should ensure that decisions are made with regard to the long-term utility, openness, integrity, and sustainability of IES, rather than the interests of any single organisation or contributor.

### Openness and Transparency

Governance documentation, decision-making structures, proposals, and release processes should be accessible and understandable. Where information cannot be published, such as some personal data, security-sensitive information, or commercially sensitive material, this should be handled proportionately and consistently.

### Clear Accountability

Groups and individuals with decision-making, maintenance, or delivery responsibilities should have defined roles, defined authority, and clear routes for escalation. Users and contributors should be able to understand who is responsible for a decision, process, repository, or artefact.

### Separation of Responsibilities

IES governance separates strategic, domain, technical, and operational responsibilities.

This separation helps ensure that decisions are taken at the appropriate level, by the appropriate group, with the appropriate expertise. It also reduces the risk that any single group or organisation controls all aspects of IES development.

### Domain Stewardship

Domain Working Groups provide the subject matter expertise needed to identify requirements, develop domain-specific extensions, and ensure that IES remains useful across real-world contexts.

### Technical Integrity

Changes should be assessed for their impact on existing ontology structures, domain extensions, interoperability, maintainability, and long-term consistency. Changes to IES Top and IES Core require particular care because they affect the common foundation on which domain-driven development depends.

### Proportional Governance

Minor, low-risk changes should not require the same level of governance as changes that affect IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1), cross-domain alignment, public release, or long-term strategic direction. Governance should provide appropriate control without creating unnecessary burden.

### Traceability

Where a material change is made, there should be a clear route from proposal, through review and approval, to implementation and release. This supports accountability, auditability, and confidence in the development of IES.

### Evolution

Governance arrangements should be reviewed and refined as IES matures, as the contributor base changes, and as the needs of users and domains develop. Changes to governance should follow the relevant governance processes and should be documented clearly.

## Governance Model

IES governance operates through a layered model.

The model is designed to balance strategic oversight, domain expertise, technical stewardship, and practical delivery. It recognises that different types of decisions require different types of authority and expertise.

The governance roles are defined in detail in the Roles section. At a high level, IES governance includes:

* **Steering Group** — provides strategic oversight and governance direction
* **Domain Working Groups** — lead domain-driven development and provide subject matter expertise
* **Technical Support and Maintenance** — supports technical integrity, repository management, and continuity of IES development

---

### IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1) and Domain-Driven Development

IES Top and IES Core together form IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1), the shared foundation used across domain-driven development. Domain-driven repositories build from, extend, or align with IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1), while domain-driven extensions are governed directly by the relevant Domain Working Groups. This model allows domains to develop material relevant to their own needs while preserving common structures across IES.

---

### Decision-Making

Decision-making within IES should occur at the lowest appropriate governance level.

In practice, this means:

* routine repository-level activity should normally be managed by the relevant repository maintainers
* domain-specific development should normally be led by the relevant Domain Working Group
* changes affecting IES Top (Layer 0) and IES Core (Layer 1) should involve the IES Top (Layer 0) Ontology Maintainers and IES Core (Layer 1) Ontology Maintainers and, where necessary, the Steering Group
* strategic, cross-domain, high-impact, or disputed matters should be escalated to the Steering Group

---

### Authoritative Source

The IES GitHub organisation is the authoritative source for IES governance documentation, approved development activity, ontology content, and official releases.

This provides a single reference point for the current approved state of IES. Other copies, forks, publications, presentations, or derived materials may support discussion, collaboration, or communication.

---

### Review and Amendment

This Charter should be reviewed periodically to ensure that it remains fit for purpose as IES evolves.

Changes to this Charter should be proposed, reviewed, and approved through the appropriate governance process. Any material change should be traceable and should preserve the principles of openness, accountability, proportionality, and public interest.
